# Awesome Half Marathon Training [![Awesome](https://awesome.re/badge.svg)](https://awesome.re)

> A curated list of evidence-based resources for half marathon training — covering physiological demands, training plans and structures, threshold-based workouts, race-day pacing and nutrition, tapering strategy, and common mistakes that prevent athletes from reaching their potential over 21.1km.

The half marathon is uniquely demanding: it sits at the intersection of aerobic capacity and lactate threshold, requiring both high aerobic base and the ability to sustain near-threshold effort for 70–120+ minutes. It rewards athletes who develop both qualities simultaneously.

## Physiology of the Half Marathon

### Energy System Demands

A half marathon is primarily an aerobic event:
- **~95% aerobic / ~5% anaerobic** energy contribution
- Raced at approximately **80–90% of VO2max** (well-trained athletes)
- Raced at approximately **85–95% of lactate threshold pace** (depending on fitness level)

### Limiting Factors

The primary physiological limiters for half marathon performance, in order of importance:

1. **Lactate threshold** — the highest pace at which lactate clearance matches production. More trainable than VO2max in experienced athletes.
2. **VO2max** — aerobic ceiling. Determines the maximum speed available; training can improve threshold fraction.
3. **Running economy** — oxygen cost per unit distance. Determines how efficiently the engine is used.
4. **Glycogen availability** — 21.1km at race intensity typically depletes 60–80% of muscle glycogen.

### Why Half Marathon Training Is Different

Unlike the marathon (wall risk, heavy fat oxidation reliance) or 5K/10K (more VO2max-limited):
- The half marathon rewards **threshold training** above all other work
- Weekly mileage above ~45 miles/week has diminishing returns vs. the marathon's continuous improvement with volume
- Speed work (VO2max intervals) is important but secondary to sustained threshold work

## Training Plans and Periodisation

### Minimum Effective Training Duration

| Goal Level | Recommended Training Period |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| First half marathon (finish) | 10–12 weeks |
| Sub-2:00 | 12–16 weeks |
| Sub-1:45 | 16–20 weeks |
| Sub-1:30 | 20–24 weeks (requires solid base) |
| Sub-1:15 | Requires years of prior development |

### Recommended Training Structure (16-week example)

**Weeks 1–6: Base Phase**
- Build weekly mileage gradually (no more than 10% per week)
- 2–3 easy runs, 1 moderate long run, 1 threshold session
- Focus: aerobic base and structural durability

**Weeks 7–12: Build Phase**
- Introduce tempo runs and race-pace intervals
- Long run extends to 15–18km
- 1–2 quality sessions per week plus easy mileage
- Focus: lactate threshold development

**Weeks 13–15: Race-Specific Phase**
- Threshold runs at or slightly faster than race pace
- Include 1 B race or tune-up 10K
- Volume near peak; quality maintained
- Focus: race-specific economy and confidence

**Week 16: Taper**
- Volume reduced by 40–50%
- Keep 1 quality session with race-pace work
- Fresh legs for race day

## Key Workout Types

### The Five Essential Workouts

**1. Easy Run (50–65% HRmax / conversational)**
- Purpose: aerobic base, recovery, volume accumulation
- Should constitute 70–80% of all training runs
- Common mistake: running too fast — this defeats the aerobic adaptation and increases fatigue

**2. Threshold Run (85–90% HRmax / comfortably hard)**
- Purpose: raise lactate threshold — the most important adaptation for half marathon
- Sustained tempo run: 20–40 min continuous at threshold
- Or cruise intervals: 3×10 min at threshold with 2 min recovery
- Rule: you should be able to speak 2–3 words at threshold; full sentences = too easy; single words = too hard

**3. Long Run (easy pace)**
- Purpose: mitochondrial development, fat oxidation, structural durability
- Distance: 80–120% of race distance (17–24km for half marathon)
- Pace: 60–75 sec/km slower than goal race pace
- Some long runs can include the last 20–30% at goal race pace (progression run)

**4. VO2max Intervals (95–100% HRmax / very hard)**
- Purpose: raise aerobic ceiling
- Protocol: 4–6 × 1km at 5K–8K pace with 90–120 sec recovery
- Or: 5 × 4 min at 3K–5K effort with equal recovery
- Frequency: 1 session/week maximum during build phase

**5. Strides / Leg Turnover**
- Purpose: neuromuscular economy, foot contact time
- 6–10 × 20 sec at 1-mile effort with full recovery
- Best after easy runs 2–3×/week
- Minimal fatigue cost; high economy return

## Building Your Aerobic Base

### The 80/20 Principle

Research across multiple endurance disciplines (running, cycling, rowing, cross-country skiing) consistently shows elite athletes spend 75–80% of training at low intensity and 15–20% at high intensity.

- **Mistake:** Most amateur runners train 60–70% at moderate intensity — hard enough to add fatigue, not hard enough to deliver threshold adaptation. This "grey zone" is the most common training error.
- **Correction:** Make easy runs genuinely easy (you should be able to hold a full conversation); make hard sessions genuinely hard.

### Mileage Guidelines by Goal

| Goal Half Marathon Time | Suggested Weekly Mileage |
|------------------------|-------------------------|
| Sub-2:30 | 25–35 km/week |
| Sub-2:00 | 35–50 km/week |
| Sub-1:45 | 50–65 km/week |
| Sub-1:30 | 65–85 km/week |
| Sub-1:15 | 85–110 km/week |

These are training phase peaks, not entry requirements. Build progressively.

## Threshold Training — The Core of Half Marathon Fitness

### What Is Threshold Pace?

Lactate threshold pace is the fastest pace you can sustain without progressive lactate accumulation. For well-trained runners:
- Approximately 85–90% of VO2max
- Approximately the pace you could race for ~60 minutes all-out
- Heart rate: 85–90% HRmax
- Breathing: hard but controlled; 3–4 word phrases possible

**Estimating threshold pace from recent race:**
- 5K pace + 15–20 sec/km ≈ threshold pace
- 10K race pace + 5–10 sec/km ≈ threshold pace
- Half marathon race pace is slightly slower than threshold pace (sub-maximal sustained effort)

### Threshold Workout Progressions

**Early build phase:**
- 3 × 10 min @ threshold with 2 min jog recovery

**Mid build phase:**
- 20–25 min continuous threshold run

**Peak build phase:**
- 35–40 min continuous threshold run (the "bread and butter" session for sub-1:30 athletes)

**Race-specific:**
- 3 × 3km at race pace with 2 min recovery — builds confidence at goal pace

## Long Run Strategy

### Purpose of the Long Run

The half marathon long run serves different purposes at different training stages:

**Aerobic base phase:**
- Easy effort, duration focus
- Increases mitochondrial density, capillary development, fat oxidation capacity
- Builds structural tolerance in connective tissue

**Build phase:**
- Progression runs: first 60% easy, final 40% at tempo/marathon effort
- Race-pace finish: last 5–8km at goal half marathon pace
- Delivers race-pace muscular stress with less overall fatigue than a full race effort

### Long Run Distance Guidance

- Maximum long run: 24–26km for most recreational half marathon athletes
- More than 26km without marathon-level training base increases injury risk without proportional benefit
- Frequency: 1 long run/week (some programmes add a mid-week medium-long run of 14–16km)

## Tapering for the Half Marathon

### Why Taper Matters

See the physiology: the half marathon depletes substantial glycogen, creates muscle microtrauma, and requires neuromuscular precision over 70–120 minutes. Arriving with uncleared fatigue directly costs time.

### Half Marathon Taper Protocol

**2 weeks out:**
- Reduce weekly volume by 25–30%
- Maintain intensity in 1–2 quality sessions
- Long run: 14–16km (easy with some race-pace finish)

**1 week out:**
- Reduce weekly volume by 40–50% vs. peak
- Keep 1 quality session mid-week (6–8km at race pace in pieces)
- No runs longer than 10km
- Final easy run: 3–5km with 4–6 strides, 2 days before race

**What not to do during taper:**
- Extra rest days (some running is better than none; detraining anxiety is normal but not real at 2 weeks)
- New shoes or new gear
- Drastic diet changes
- Panic extra training

## Race-Day Pacing Strategy

### Even-Split vs. Negative-Split

**The science is clear:** an even-split or slight negative-split (second half 0–30 sec faster than first) produces faster finish times than positive-split racing (going out too fast).

- Biochemical reason: early lactic acid accumulation from a fast first half forces pace reduction in the second half that outweighs any time gained early
- Most amateur half marathoners run the second half 3–5% slower than the first — indicating they went out too hard

**Target pacing:**
- First 3km: 5 seconds per km slower than goal race pace
- Km 4–18: goal race pace
- Final 3km: race by effort; push if you have remaining capacity

### Heart Rate as Pacing Guide

| First 5km | Middle Km | Final 5km |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| 82–85% HRmax | 86–90% HRmax | 90–94% HRmax |

If you hit 90% HRmax in the first 5km on a flat course in normal conditions, you are racing too hard.

## Race-Day Nutrition

### Carbohydrate Loading

For a 90–120 minute race, moderate carbohydrate loading is beneficial:
- **48 hours before:** Increase carbohydrate intake to 8–10g/kg body mass/day
- **24 hours before:** Continue high-carb intake; reduce fibre to minimise GI risk
- **Race morning:** 2–3g/kg carbohydrate 2–3 hours before start

### In-Race Fuelling

For athletes finishing under 90 minutes:
- 30–45g carbohydrate per hour is sufficient
- 1 gel at ~45–50 minutes is standard practice

For athletes finishing 90–120+ minutes:
- 45–60g carbohydrate per hour
- 1 gel at 30–35 minutes and 1 gel at 60–70 minutes
- Practice all gels and fluids in training — no new products on race day

### Hydration Strategy

- Drink to thirst rather than to a schedule
- Aid stations: take a cup; drink what you need, pour remainder on your head in heat
- No plain water in isolation for athletes >90 min — take electrolytes with fluids

## Common Training Mistakes

### 1. Too Much Medium Intensity

Training every run at "moderate hard" pace — too fast to build aerobic base, too slow to deliver threshold adaptation. The solution is polarised training: truly easy runs and truly hard quality sessions.

### 2. Too Little Threshold Work

5K and 10K specialists who step up to the half marathon often fail to adapt their training. The half marathon requires sustained threshold work, not just interval sessions.

### 3. Too Short a Build Phase

Many athletes begin a 10-week programme without adequate base. 10 weeks is sufficient to peak existing fitness — it is not sufficient to build new fitness. A 16–20 week plan from a base of 30+ km/week is more appropriate.

### 4. Race-Day Nutrition Errors

- Starting too fast, depleting glycogen early, hitting a wall at km 16–18
- Not practising race-day nutrition in training
- Underestimating the importance of even moderate carbohydrate loading

### 5. Neglecting Recovery

Easy runs must be genuinely easy. Runners who cannot pace easy runs correctly benefit from training by heart rate rather than by pace — particularly in hot, humid conditions or at altitude.

## Strength and Injury Prevention

### Minimum Effective Dose

2×/week strength work during the base phase, 1×/week during peak training, reduces injury rates without compromising endurance adaptation.

**Priority exercises for half marathon runners:**
- Single-leg calf raises (Achilles and plantar fascia protection)
- Nordic hamstring curl (hamstring injury prevention)
- Hip abductor work (IT band and patellofemoral protection)
- Copenhagen plank (adductor and hip stability)

### Managing the Most Common Injuries

| Injury | Prevention | Training Modification |
|--------|-----------|---------------------|
| Shin splints | Gradual mileage increase; calf strength | Reduce volume; switch to soft surfaces |
| IT band syndrome | Hip abductor strengthening | Avoid downhill running; address hip weakness |
| Plantar fasciitis | Calf loading; avoid over-stretching | Avoid barefoot; add cushioning; load the tendon |
| Patellofemoral pain | VMO and glute strengthening | Reduce downhill; address hip weakness |

## Calculators & Tools

- [Marathon Wall Predictor — NorthLine](https://winsport.us/tools/sports/marathon-wall-predictor) — Predict glycogen depletion risk and pacing sustainability for your half marathon using your training data. Useful for identifying whether your current fitness supports your goal pace.
- [McMillan Running Calculator](https://www.mcmillanrunning.com/) — Equivalent race time predictions between distances. Use to set realistic half marathon goals from recent 5K/10K times.
- [Daniels' Running Pace Calculator](https://runsmartproject.com/calculator/) — VDOT-based pace calculator; provides training paces for easy, tempo, interval, and repetition zones.
- [Runalyze — Race Performance Analysis](https://runalyze.com/) — Free training analytics platform with race time prediction, VO2max estimation, and training load tracking.
- [Intervals.icu](https://intervals.icu/) — Free training analytics with ATL/CTL/TSB tracking. Ideal for monitoring half marathon training load distribution.
- [Strava — Fitness & Freshness](https://www.strava.com/) — Built-in training load chart (Fitness/Fatigue/Form) for premium users. Useful for timing your taper correctly.
- [Running in the USA — Half Marathon Calendar](https://www.runningintheusa.com/) — Searchable half marathon race calendar for finding target races at appropriate distances from your training location.
- - [Mile Pace Calculator](https://milepacecalculator.com/) - Free pace calculators for runners. Find your mile pace, treadmill pace, marathon pace, splits and finish times.

## Books & Resources

- **"Daniels' Running Formula"** — Jack Daniels (3rd ed., 2014). The training pace system used by most serious runners. Phase-based plans from novice to elite.
- **"Advanced Marathoning"** — Pete Pfitzinger & Scott Douglas (3rd ed., 2019). Half marathon content is directly applicable; superior threshold training structure.
- **"80/20 Running"** — Matt Fitzgerald (2014). Evidence-based case for polarised training. Practical plan structure for all distances including the half marathon.
- **"Run Faster from the 5K to the Marathon"** — Brad Hudson & Matt Fitzgerald (2008). Adaptive training philosophy with strong threshold work structure.
- [Science of Running — Steve Magness](https://www.scienceofrunning.com/) — Advanced training physiology applied to half marathon and marathon programming.
